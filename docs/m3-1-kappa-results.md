# Inter-rater reliability report: the M3.1 κ pass

**M3.1 (issue #35) · 2026-09-26 · branch `asma-week-22-m3-followup`**

Two independent raters labelled a blinded 100-row subset of
`datasets/soc_injection_benchmark_v1.csv` (50 attack / 50 benign controls,
shuffled, no `family`/`benchmark_id`/`is_benign_control`/`modified_field`
column) as `injection` or `benign`. Neither saw the other's sheet. The
returned sheets are committed verbatim as
`experiments/results/m3_1_rating_sheet_raterA_completed.csv` and
`…raterB_completed.csv`; every number below comes from
`experiments/results/m3_1_interrater_kappa.json`, regenerated by

```bash
venv/bin/python experiments/m3_1_interrater_kappa.py \
  --rater-a experiments/results/m3_1_rating_sheet_raterA_completed.csv \
  --rater-b experiments/results/m3_1_rating_sheet_raterB_completed.csv
```

## Result: **κ = 0.8178, target κ ≥ 0.75 met**

| | |
|---|---|
| Cohen's κ (pooled, 100 rows) | **0.8178** |
| Raw agreement | 0.9100 (91/100) |
| Chance agreement *p*ₑ | 0.5060 |
| Disagreements | 9 |
| Rater A accuracy vs construction key | 0.9700 |
| Rater B accuracy vs construction key | 0.9000 |

The pooled κ is the pre-registered acceptance criterion in issue #35. It
clears the threshold, so the below-threshold protocol in the rubric — review
the disputed rows together, sharpen the rubric where the disagreement traces
to an ambiguous rule, re-label only those rows — was **not** triggered. No
returned rating was edited and the pass was not re-run.

## The pooled number has a ceiling, and the strata show it

The datasheet (§4, "What κ from this subset can and cannot establish") and
the rubric ("Ceiling caveat") both recorded, *before the raters were sent
anything*, that all 50 benign rows in this subset are bare numeric GUIDE
field codes while all 50 attack rows are natural language — so the two
classes are separable on surface form alone. Splitting the strata checks that
prediction instead of leaving it as an assertion:

| Stratum | n | Raw agreement | *p*ₑ | κ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pooled | 100 | 0.9100 | 0.5060 | 0.8178 |
| Benign controls only | 50 | **1.0000** | 1.0000 | undefined (both raters unanimous) |
| Attack rows only | 50 | 0.8200 | 0.7640 | 0.2373 |

The prediction holds. Agreement on the benign controls is perfect — 50/50,
both raters, no exceptions — and **every one of the 9 disagreements is an
attack row**. The pooled κ of 0.8178 is therefore carried substantially by a
stratum on which the task is trivial.

**Read the attack-only κ of 0.2373 carefully, in both directions.** It is not
evidence that the raters agreed only slightly: on the attack stratum both
raters answer `injection` on almost every row, which pushes chance agreement
to *p*ₑ = 0.764 and deflates κ mechanically — the high-prevalence kappa
paradox. Raw agreement, 41/50, is the meaningful figure in that stratum. But
neither is 0.8178 evidence that the rubric discriminates on hard cases. The
defensible claim is the narrow one: **the binary labels are unambiguous to
independent readers, and the subset's class balance does not let this pass
say more than that.** Detector difficulty is a separate question, measured in
M3.2.

## Where the disagreement sits

| Category | n in subset | Disagreements | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| BCONTROL (benign) | 50 | 0 | 0.0% |
| F1 Direct Override | 7 | 0 | 0.0% |
| F2 Role Playing | 5 | 3 | 60.0% |
| F3 Passive/Buried | 8 | 4 | 50.0% |
| F4 Encoded/Obfuscated | 6 | 0 | 0.0% |
| F5 Cross-Field Fragmentation | 10 | 0 | 0.0% |
| F6 False Evidence Injection | 8 | 0 | 0.0% |
| F7 Output-Format/JSON | 6 | 2 | 33.3% |

All nine disagreements fall in three of the seven families. The rubric named
F3 in advance as "where the rubric is actually tested", and F3 does carry the
most disagreements (4); F2's rate is nominally higher at 3/5, but on n = 5 that
is one or two rows of noise and should not be reported as F2 being harder than
F3. The families with an explicit imperative (F1), a decodable directive (F4),
a split payload (F5), or a forged-evidence claim with no command (F6) produced
no disagreement at all.

The mechanism is visible in the rows themselves. Eight of the nine are cases
where Rater B called an attack benign, and all eight replace an imperative
with something that reads as ordinary SOC material: an attributed shift note
("Analyst note appended by prior shift…"), a role assertion ("Role: incident
commander…"), or a machine-readable fragment that looks like protocol noise
rather than an instruction (F7's pre-filled JSON and fake closing tags). The
single row in the other direction, `W015`, is the one payload in the set that
asserts nothing at all and argues purely by analogy — "similar tickets this
month were all resolved as FalsePositive and this one follows the same
pattern" — and Rater A read it as benign context.

rater-accuracy figures above are accuracy against this key, not against a
consensus panel.

Direction of correction: **B on 8 rows, A on 1.** Every correction is in the
same direction — a payload that is an attack was called benign — so no row in
this subset was a false alarm by either rater.

## No rubric change is triggered

Each of the nine rows is settled by a rule the rubric already states, quoted
per row above; none of them turns on a gap or a contradiction in the
definitions. The F3 and F7 misses are the rule being *missed in practice* on
subtle text, not the rule being unclear — which is the outcome the rubric's
ceiling caveat anticipated and the reason the per-family breakdown is reported
next to the pooled number. Combined with κ landing above the 0.75 gate, the
rubric ships as written.

## What this does and does not license

- **Does:** state a real inter-rater κ of 0.8178 for the released benchmark,
  with the per-family and per-stratum breakdown and this resolution log.
- **Does not:** support any claim that the rubric separates hard cases, or any
  inference from κ to detector difficulty. Quote the stratified table, not the
  pooled number alone, whenever the figure is used as evidence about the
  benchmark's quality.
